# Econ Abstract Writing

English | [简体中文](SKILL.zh.md)

Once the results, the paper's complete argument, and the Introduction are in place, guide the author
through five questions, then draft or review the abstract using
**What → How → Findings → Findings → Findings (WHFFF)**.
Converse in the author's preferred language; write the abstract in English by default.
The language of these instructions does not determine the language of the conversation.

## 1. Write the abstract last

At the outset, briefly remind the author once: **Establish the results, develop the complete story,
and write the Introduction before writing the abstract.**
A complete story connects the research question, method, findings, and contribution so the author
can explain what the paper says. The rest of the manuscript need not have received its final polish.

Infer readiness from the supplied materials; do not ask for information already available.

| Available material | How to begin |
|---|---|
| Main results, a complete story, and a written Introduction, but no abstract | Ask the five questions below, then draft. |
| A complete paper draft | Read the Introduction, research design, main results, and relevant tables or conclusion. Extract the five answers with source locations, and ask the author to check the central question and selection of findings. |
| An existing abstract | Map its content to the five questions and carry out the requested review or revision. Consult any supplied manuscript or results for claims that need checking. |
| An idea or scattered results, without a complete story and Introduction | Organize what is known and identify the key gaps. Recommend completing the story and Introduction first; deliver a content outline at this stage, rather than a finished abstract. |

If the author has already supplied the five answers, or explicitly asks you to select content from
the manuscript and draft directly, proceed using that information and authorization without another
formal confirmation. Reviewing an existing abstract does not require the author to supply the full paper.

## 2. Ask the author five questions

Encourage the author to explain the substance in Chinese or English before polishing sentences.
Present the questions together, reuse answers already given, and ask only about gaps that affect
the abstract's content. When working from a full paper, present the same five items for the author to check.

1. **W — Core question:** What is the paper's central research question? Which people, change, or economic relationship does it examine?
2. **H — Key method:** What is the most important method used to answer that question? Identify the key research design, data, or model.
3. **F1 — Direct answer:** What is the most important answer to the research question? Include a meaningful magnitude when available.
4. **F2 — Explanation:** Why does this result arise? What evidence does the paper provide about the mechanism?
5. **F3 — Further finding:** Beyond the main result and its mechanism, what else should the reader remember? What new information does it add?

The default progression of the three Fs is:
**What did you find? → Why does it happen? → What else did you find?**

- F1 answers W directly. Preserve the main direction, magnitude, or theoretical proposition and its conditions.
- F2 prioritizes the mechanism behind the main result. Distinguish a tested mechanism, evidence consistent with a mechanism, and the author's conjecture; retain the original strength of evidence.
- F3 selects important heterogeneity, contrasting results, trade-offs, longer-term effects, or economic consequences such as welfare from the paper's actual findings.
  Favor the result that most adds to or changes the reader's understanding; avoid a generic policy-implications ending.

If there is no mechanism evidence, say so and use another substantive finding for F2.
If the materials contain only one or two distinct findings, shorten the structure while preserving
necessary magnitudes, conditions, and explanation. Do not invent results to fill three Fs.
For theory papers, findings may be propositions, equilibrium properties, or conditions; the method
may be the key model assumptions or proof approach.

## 3. Settle the content before writing English prose

Check that the five answers form a clear argument: W is focused, H explains how it is answered,
F1 responds to W, and F2 and F3 each add information.
Compare them with the Introduction's claims and the actual results to preserve the contribution's focus.

For repetitive findings, identify the overlap and look for more informative content in the supplied materials.
If the question and results do not match, ask the author to clarify the intended research question.
Do not conceal that mismatch through smoother prose.
If the text conflicts with a table, or the Introduction with the results, identify the specific conflict
and ask the author to resolve it before using the disputed claim.

Start with five-sentence WHFFF, reserving room for findings before allocating space to the question and method.
Allow W and H to share a sentence, a necessary method explanation to take two sentences, and the number
of findings sentences to increase or decrease with the content.
For alternative arrangements, read [Abstract patterns](references/abstract_patterns.md).
For examples of content selection or revision, read [Teaching examples](references/examples.md).

Use the author's word limit or the submission system's requirement.
If only a journal name is given, verify its current official limit for the relevant article type before
editing to that limit. If you cannot verify it, ask the author for the requirement; do not treat a historical
median as a submission limit.
Without a specified target, produce a compact first draft of about five sentences and report the actual
word count. Do not pack long compound sentences together merely to meet a sentence count.

## 4. English prose and fidelity to the research

- State the research subject, action, and result directly. Explain the key design needed to understand the findings, retaining other method details only as needed.
- When a meaningful magnitude is available, preserve its unit, comparison group, and time horizon. Do not force numerical results into a theoretical proposition.
- Make successive findings add information instead of restating the main result. Connect sentences through relationships that actually exist in the research.
- Remove generic background, repeated summaries, and uninformative self-defense. Preserve conditions, uncertainty, and scope that affect the meaning of the results.
  Use expressions such as `suggest`, `consistent with`, and `in contrast` according to their meaning; do not ban them through a word list.
- Preserve percentages versus percentage points, directions, subjects, causal strength, mechanism evidence, and author intent. Do not import facts, numbers, or wording from the examples.
  Ask about missing results instead of filling them with plausible values.

## 5. Deliver drafts, revisions, and reviews

**Drafting or revision:** By default, provide one English abstract, its word count, and a short explanation
of the main content choices or changes. If the author wants only the abstract, provide only the abstract.
Add a sentence-to-W/H/F1/F2/F3 mapping when requested or useful for teaching.

**Review only:** Map the existing abstract's functions and prioritize specific problems that most affect
understanding, quoting the relevant sentence and suggesting a change.
Preserve sentences that work. Do not mark an abstract wrong simply because it departs from five sentences,
and do not automatically produce a revision the author has not requested.

Before delivery, check against the supplied research materials:

- Does F1 answer the central question, and do the later Fs add information?
- Are numbers, comparison groups, scope, and strength of evidence preserved?
- Is the abstract consistent with the Introduction and main results, and are the supplied materials sufficient to check this?
- Does the draft meet the known word limit, and can background, methods, or the ending be shortened without losing needed content?

When only an abstract is available, explain that the review covers structure and expression, and flag
specific factual uncertainties for the author to check.
Keep that scope note in the feedback; do not insert process descriptions or generic disclaimers into the abstract.

## Research basis

This method combines AI Writing Lab's structural observations of 4,250 economics Top5 abstracts
with its chosen author workflow.
For the statistics, journal differences, or scope of the method, read
[Corpus evidence and method origins](references/corpus_evidence.md).
The five questions and WHFFF are writing aids used by this skill. The corpus statistics do not show
that all abstracts follow one sequence, or establish effects on writing quality or publication probability.
