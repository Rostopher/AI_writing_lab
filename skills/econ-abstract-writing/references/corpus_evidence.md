# Corpus evidence and method origins

[简体中文](zh/corpus_evidence.md) | English

Read this only to explain the method's basis, scope, or journal differences.
Routine drafting does not require rerunning the corpus analysis.

## What the study covers

AI Writing Lab annotated sentence functions and conducted descriptive analysis of 4,250 available abstracts
from AER, JPE, Econometrica, REStud, and QJE over 2015–2026.
The 2026 coverage is partial, and the corpus is limited to material actually acquired, not every paper
published by these journals during the period.
The full run used a single annotation model and was conducted on 2026-09-09.

W denotes the research question or subject, H the method, F findings, and Y significance.
One sentence can serve several functions.

| Observation | Implication for organizing an abstract |
|---|---|
| Findings account for 49.4% of the text on average, calculated within each abstract and then averaged. | Select the findings to report before allocating space to background and methods. |
| The median is 5 sentences; abstracts with 4–6 sentences account for 65.5%. | Five sentences can be a starting arrangement, adjusted to the content and requirements. |
| W/H generally appear before F, and functions often share a sentence. | Establish the question and research approach before expanding the results; allow combined functions. |
| The complete W-H-F-F-F sequence accounts for 3.6% of five-sentence abstracts. | WHFFF is a practical organizing aid, not a complete sequence followed by most papers. |
| Median word counts differ: AER/JPE 100, Econometrica 136, REStud 140.5, QJE 160. | Allocate space for the target journal and article type. These are historical sample medians, not submission limits. |

F in the annotations denotes a findings function; F1/F2/F3 in this skill are roles assigned for writing.
The corpus does not establish a fixed rule that the second finding must report a mechanism
or that the third must report welfare.

## The author workflow chosen for this project

“Write the abstract last,” “complete the results, story, and Introduction first,” and “ask the author five questions”
are the workflow adopted by this project.
Using F1 to answer the question, prioritizing mechanism evidence in F2, and choosing a further finding for F3
are editorial methods for selecting content, with adjustments for the actual research.

Distinguish those workflow choices from the corpus observations above.
The corpus does not show that a structure increases publication probability,
and the first version of the skill has not undergone a comparative evaluation of writing outcomes.

## Sources and further use

For the complete method, numbers, and limitations, see the repository's
[full descriptive report on abstract structure (Chinese)](https://github.com/Rostopher/AI_writing_lab/blob/master/notes/data/abstract_structure_full_report_v03.md).
The public link remains a source pointer when the skill is installed independently;
writing does not depend on the repository's data files.

The report records limitations including single-model full-corpus annotation, some within-paper annotation
disagreements awaiting human review, gaps in acquired data, and a specialized review of stock phrases,
terminology, and identification explanations that has not yet been run.
Do not present stylistic editing advice as a finding validated by that corpus.

The skill includes summaries of key statistics and fictional teaching examples.
It does not bundle the original paper abstracts or per-paper annotation caches.
